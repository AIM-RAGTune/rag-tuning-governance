\begin{abstract}
RAGTune is an open-source RAG governance and promotion-control framework. It does not replace RAG evaluation or observability tools; it consumes evaluator outputs and operational telemetry, then emits auditable promotion, rejection, blocked, or inconclusive decisions. This systems/methods draft summarizes public-mini reproduction, Docker runtime hardening, external evaluator adapter demos, selector ablation stress testing, artifact integrity verification, sanitized AIM hardware characterization, and mixed generative validation evidence. RAG Compass superiority, human validation, official platform benchmarking, production readiness, stable generative cost/latency superiority, and hallucination elimination are unsupported.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
RAGTune addresses the gap between RAG evaluation metrics and deployment promotion decisions. It provides a conservative governance layer for comparing policies under quality, cost, latency, provenance, and risk constraints.

\section{Problem Statement}
RAG systems often produce multiple plausible policy candidates. A promotion controller must decide whether a candidate should be promoted, blocked, rejected, or marked inconclusive without overstating the evidence.

\section{Governance Model}
RAGTune represents each candidate policy through sanitized metrics, operational telemetry, claim boundaries, and reproducibility metadata. Promotion is allowed only when predeclared gates pass.

\section{Policy Promotion and Refusal Taxonomy}
The taxonomy includes promote, block, reject, inconclusive, fail-closed, partial, and unavailable results. Mixed and fail-closed outcomes are preserved as scientific evidence rather than hidden.

\section{Claim-Boundary Validation}
The validator checks raw-data exclusion, secret exclusion, private-path exclusion, CRAG redistribution boundaries, unsupported-claim language, and machine-readable result classes.

\section{External Evaluator Adapter Architecture}
RAGTune does not replace RAG evaluators. It consumes evaluator-style exports, normalizes metrics, and converts those metrics into promotion-control decisions.

\section{Public Mini Reproduction}
The public-mini path is synthetic and requires no raw CRAG, HotpotQA, generator credentials, or local private data. It exercises the finite governance job and produces sanitized artifacts.

\section{Selector Ablation Matrix}
Selector ablations compare quality-only, cost-only, latency-only, random, static, RAG Compass optional, governed, guarded, and oracle-style selectors. The purpose is to expose unsafe promotions, not to claim universal superiority.

\section{Generative Validation and Fail-Closed Results}
Generative validation remains mixed. Some bounded local CRAG slices showed cost or latency reductions, while independent offsets and quality-risk guardrails exposed generated-quality loss. Guardrail v2 failed closed under held-out quality loss. HotpotQA audits provide a nonconstant generated-quality signal but do not establish broad generative governance superiority.

\section{Docker and Deployment Readiness}
The repository includes a hardened Docker public-mini runtime and cloud-agnostic templates. These support deployable-tool evaluation and operator workflow design, not production validation or official cloud benchmarking.

\section{AIM Hardware Characterization}
The AIM hardware matrix records sanitized local runtime facts. It excludes hostnames, IP addresses, serial numbers, private paths, secrets, and full environment dumps. It is not official platform benchmarking.

\section{Limitations}
RAG Compass superiority is unsupported. Human validation is unsupported. Official platform benchmarking is unsupported. Production readiness is unsupported. Stable generative cost/latency superiority is unsupported. Raw licensed datasets must be obtained from their approved sources.

\section{Reproducibility}
Reproducibility artifacts include fresh-clone drill outputs, Docker public-mini validation, release-candidate manifests, checksums, verify-run artifacts, selector stress tests, and sanitized synthesis reports.

\section{Conclusion}
RAGTune is best supported as an evidence-preserving RAG policy governance framework. Its contribution is disciplined promotion control, refusal discipline, and reproducible claim-boundary validation.
